# Content Safety

Acento teaches real regional Spanish with context and care.

## Rules

- Do not invent slang.
- Do not present slang as universally appropriate.
- Do not over-romanticize Dominican culture.
- Do not use stereotypes.
- Do not flatten all Dominican speakers into one speech pattern.
- Always include standard Spanish alongside regional phrases.
- Always explain that usage varies by region, age, class, relationship, and context.
- Use Dominican Spanish as a regional track, not the only correct way to speak.

## Required Context For Slang

Every slang or context-sensitive phrase should include:

- Meaning.
- Common English equivalent.
- Pronunciation guide.
- Formality level.
- Slang level.
- Region.
- Usage notes.
- Cultural notes.
- Who commonly uses it.
- Safe contexts.
- Risky contexts.
- Example sentence.
- Standard Spanish equivalent.
- English translation.
- Related phrases.

## Sensitive Areas

### Flirting

Flirting content must be respectful, non-explicit, consent-centered, and practical.

Do not teach:

- Harassment.
- Pressure.
- Catcalling.
- Manipulation.
- Explicit sexual content.

### Terms Of Affection

Terms like `mi amor` must not be taught only as romantic. They may be romantic, affectionate, customer-service warmth, or culturally casual depending on speaker and context.

Learners must be warned that using affectionate terms toward strangers, coworkers, service workers, or women they do not know can sound invasive or disrespectful.
